Redesigning Ivy Workflow Into a Unified Business Management

Overview

Ordo helps interior designers manage proposals, invoices, sourcing, and reporting but suffered from navigation clutter, redundant flows, and visual inconsistency. This redesign aimed to simplify workflows, centralize finance features, and make reporting more discoverable and actionable—all while maintaining a polished, modern aesthetic.

Problems

Designers struggled to locate proposals, invoices, and reporting tools due to overlapping categories. The main features were not clear

Key flows (e.g., sending an invoice, tracking payments) had unnecessary friction. The steps were not consistent across all the screens

Color contrast, text size, and interaction targets were not fully optimized for accessibility. This limited inclusivity and could frustrate users with visual

Buttons, cards, and form elements varied across screens. This inconsistency confused users and disrupted the sense of a cohesive, professional product.

Solutions

We introduced a simplified information architecture by grouping features under distinct, task-oriented categories (e.g., “Proposals,” “Invoices,” “Reports”).

We mapped the end-to-end journey for each core task and identified redundant clicks and inconsistent patterns. The flows were redesigned to reduce the cognitive load.

We mapped the end-to-end journey for each core task and identified redundant clicks and inconsistent patterns. The flows were to reduce the cognitive load.

We applied WCAG AA standards across typography, color, and components. Contrast ratios were adjusted for clarity, and text sizes were increased in key content areas.

We applied WCAG AA standards across typography, color, and components. Contrast ratios were adjusted for clarity, and text sizes were increased

We developed a unified design system that defined core components (buttons, form fields, cards, modals) with clear usage guidelines.

Research & Insights

To deeply understand where the product experience was breaking down, I conducted a heuristic evaluation of all core flows, including proposal management, invoicing, payment tracking, and reporting.

The evaluation was guided by Nielsen’s usability heuristics and modern accessibility guidelines. Each screen was analyzed for clarity, consistency, error prevention, and inclusivity.
